## Lesson 1 — To Be (am/is/are) + A/An

### Part 1: I am, you are, she is

Use am, is or are to say who someone is, what something is, how someone feels or where someone is. Use am with I. Use is with he, she, it and one person or thing. Use are with you, we, they and two or more people or things. In speaking we often use short forms: I'm, she's, they're.

| Person | be | Short form |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | I'm |
| you | are | you're |
| he / she / it | is | he's / she's / it's |
| we | are | we're |
| they | are | they're |

Examples:

- I am a student.
- You are my friend.
- Tom is ten years old.
- It is cold today.
- We're in the classroom.
- Anna and Sam are at home.

🇺🇿 I bilan am, he / she / it bilan is, you / we / they bilan are ishlatiladi. Bu o‘zbek tilidagi -man, -san, -dir qo‘shimchalariga o‘xshaydi: I am a student — Men talabaman.

**Practise**

1. [Choose] I ___ a student. — **am** / is / are  
   *Hint: With I, always use am.*
2. [Choose] She ___ my sister. — am / **is** / are  
   *Hint: With he, she and it, use is.*
3. [Write] We ___ in the park. (am / is / are) → **are**  
   *Hint: With we, use are.*
4. [Write] Tom and Lucy ___ friends. (am / is / are) → **are**  
   *Hint: Tom and Lucy = they, so use are.*
5. [Put in order] is / The / big / house → **The house is big.**  
   *Hint: Start with The house, then is.*

### Part 2: Negatives and questions

To make a negative, put not after am, is or are: I am not, she is not. Short forms: I'm not, isn't, aren't. To ask a question, put am, is or are before the person: Are you OK? Is he at home? Answer with a short answer: Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.

| Negative | Question | Short answer |
|---|---|---|
| I am not (I'm not) tired. | Am I late? | Yes, you are. / No, you aren't. |
| He is not (He isn't) here. | Is he at home? | Yes, he is. / No, he isn't. |
| They are not (They aren't) ready. | Are they at school? | Yes, they are. / No, they aren't. |

Examples:

- I am not tired.
- Sam isn't at school today.
- We aren't hungry.
- Are you a teacher? — Yes, I am.
- Is it hot today? — No, it isn't.
- Where are your books?

🇺🇿 Inkor gapda am, is, are dan keyin not qo‘yiladi: I am not tired — Men charchamaganman. So‘roq gapda am, is, are gapning boshiga chiqadi: Are you OK?

**Practise**

1. [Choose] He ___ a doctor. He is a teacher. — **isn't** / aren't / am not  
   *Hint: With he, use is. The negative is isn't (is not).*
2. [Write] I ___ not hungry. → **am**  
   *Hint: With I, use am: I am not.*
3. [Choose] ___ you happy? — Am / Is / **Are**  
   *Hint: In a question, are comes first with you.*

   *Hint: In a question, is comes before she.*
5. [Put in order] they / Are / school / at → **Are they at school?**  
   *Hint: A question starts with Are, then they.*

### Part 3: a or an?

Use a or an before one person or thing. Use an before a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u): an apple, an egg. Use a before other sounds: a cat, a book. Look at the next word: an old car, but a new car. Use a or an with jobs too: She is a doctor.

| a + other sounds | an + a, e, i, o, u sounds |
|---|---|
| a dog | an apple |
| a pen | an egg |
| a big house | an old house |
| a nurse | an engineer |

Examples:

- It is a dog.
- This is an orange.
- My mum is a nurse.
- He is an actor.
- It is an old car.
- It is a new car.

🇺🇿 a / an — bitta degan ma’noni beradi. Unli tovush (a, e, i, o, u) oldidan an, boshqa so‘zlar oldidan a qo‘yiladi. Kasb oldidan ham qo‘yiladi: She is a doctor — U shifokor.

## Lesson 2 — Subject Pronouns + Possessive Adjectives

### Part 1: I, you, he, she, it, we, they

A subject pronoun takes the place of a name. It comes before the verb (am, is, are). Use he for a man or a boy, she for a woman or a girl, it for a thing or an animal, and they for two or more people or things.

| One | Two or more |
|---|---|
| I | we |
| you | you |
| he / she / it | they |

Examples:

- Tom is my friend. He is twelve.
- Anna is a nurse. She is very kind.
- This is my bag. It is new.
- Sam and I are in Class 5. We are classmates.
- The books are on the table. They are heavy.

🇺🇿 O‘zbek tilida bitta “u” bor, ingliz tilida esa uchta: he — erkak yoki o‘g‘il bola, she — ayol yoki qiz bola, it — narsa yoki hayvon.

**Practise**

1. [Choose] Lucy is my cousin. ___ is ten. — He / **She** / It  
   *Hint: Lucy is a girl, so use she.*
2. [Choose] My dad is a driver. ___ is at work. — **He** / She / They  
   *Hint: Dad is a man, so use he.*
3. [Choose] The cat is black. ___ is very small. — He / She / **It**  
   *Hint: Use it for an animal or a thing.*
4. [Write] Ben and Kate are students. ___ are in my class. (Ben and Kate) → **They**  
   *Hint: Two people (not you or me) = they.*
5. [Write] My friend and I are in the park. ___ are happy. (my friend and I) → **We**  
   *Hint: Another person + I = we.*

### Part 2: my, your, his, her, its, our, their

Possessive adjectives show who something belongs to. They come before a noun: my book, her phone. They are the same for one thing or many things: my book, my books. Be careful: his is for a man or a boy, her is for a woman or a girl.

| Subject pronoun | Possessive adjective | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | my | my bag |
| you | your | your bag |
| he | his | his bag |
| she | her | her bag |
| it | its | its bed (a dog's bed) |
| we | our | our bag |
| they | their | their bag |

Examples:

- This is my bag.
- What is your name?
- Tom is with his mother.
- Her cat is white.
- We are in our classroom.
- Their house is very big.

🇺🇿 Bu so‘zlar narsa kimniki ekanini bildiradi va otdan oldin keladi: my book — mening kitobim. his — erkak kishiniki, her — ayol kishiniki.

**Practise**

1. [Choose] I am Tom. ___ name is Tom. — **My** / I / Me  
   *Hint: Before a noun (name), use my.*
2. [Choose] Kate is with ___ brother. — his / **her** / its  
   *Hint: Kate is a girl, so use her.*
3. [Choose] Mr Brown is in ___ car. — **his** / her / their  
   *Hint: Mr Brown is a man, so use his.*
4. [Write] We are in ___ classroom. (we) → **our**  
   *Hint: we → our.*
5. [Correct sentence] **They are with their teacher.** ✓ · They are with they teacher. ✗ · They are with there teacher. ✗  
   *Hint: they → their (it belongs to them).*

### Part 3: he or his? it's or its?

Before am, is, are (a verb), use I, he, she, we, they. Before a noun, use my, his, her, our, their. Be careful with it's and its: it's = it is. its = belonging to it (no apostrophe).

| Before a verb | Before a noun |
|---|---|
| He is my uncle. | His car is red. |
| She is a singer. | Her name is Mia. |
| They are twins. | Their names are Jack and Sam. |
| It's a dog. (it is) | Its name is Max. |

Examples:

- She is a singer. Her name is Mia.
- He is my uncle. His car is red.
- They are twins. Their names are Jack and Sam.
- It's a dog. Its name is Max.
- We are in Class 7. Our teacher is Mrs Green.

🇺🇿 Fe’l (am, is, are) oldidan I, he, she, they, ot oldidan esa my, his, her, their ishlatiladi. it’s = it is, its esa “uning” degani.

**Practise**

3. [Choose] This is a bird. ___ wings are blue. — It's / **Its**  
   *Hint: its = belonging to it. it's = it is.*
4. [Write] Jack and Mia are my friends. ___ house is near my school. (Jack and Mia) → **Their**  
   *Hint: Before a noun (house), they → their.*
5. [Correct sentence] **Our teacher is from Canada.** ✓ · We teacher is from Canada. ✗ · Us teacher is from Canada. ✗  
   *Hint: Before a noun (teacher), we → our.*

## Lesson 3 — Singular and Plural Nouns

### Part 1: -s and -es

Singular means one. Plural means two or more. Most nouns add -s: book → books. Nouns that end in -s, -ss, -sh, -ch or -x add -es: bus → buses, box → boxes. Some nouns that end in -o add -es too: tomato → tomatoes. After a number (two, three, ten…) the noun is plural: two books.

| Ending | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| most nouns | + s | a cat → two cats |
| -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x | + es | a bus → buses, a dish → dishes, a watch → watches |
| -o (some nouns) | + es | a tomato → tomatoes, a potato → potatoes |

Examples:

- one pen → three pens
- a class → two classes
- a brush → brushes
- a box → five boxes
- My shoes are new.
- The buses are full.

🇺🇿 Ko‘plikda otga -s yoki -es qo‘shiladi (books — kitoblar). Diqqat: o‘zbek tilidan farqli, sondan keyin ham -s qo‘shiladi: two books — ikkita kitob.

**Practise**

1. [Choose] one apple, two ___ — apple / **apples** / applees  
   *Hint: Most nouns just add -s.*
2. [Write] one box, three ___ → **boxes**  
   *Hint: Nouns that end in -x add -es.*
3. [Write] one watch, two ___ → **watches**  
   *Hint: Nouns that end in -ch add -es.*
4. [Choose] The ___ are on the table. — dish / dishs / **dishes**  
   *Hint: Nouns that end in -sh add -es.*
5. [Correct sentence] **Tom is ten years old.** ✓ · Tom is ten year old. ✗ · Tom is ten years olds. ✗  
   *Hint: After a number like ten, the noun is plural: ten years.*

### Part 2: -y → -ies and -f → -ves

If a noun ends in a consonant + y, change y to i and add -es: baby → babies, city → cities. If it ends in a vowel + y (ay, ey, oy), just add -s: day → days, key → keys, boy → boys. Many nouns that end in -f or -fe change to -ves: leaf → leaves, knife → knives.

| Ending | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| consonant + y | y → ies | a baby → babies, a city → cities |
| vowel + y (ay, ey, oy) | + s | a day → days, a key → keys, a boy → boys |
| -f / -fe | → ves | a leaf → leaves, a knife → knives, a shelf → shelves |

Examples:

- one city → two cities
- a lady → three ladies
- a day → seven days
- a toy → many toys
- a shelf → two shelves
- The leaves are yellow.

🇺🇿 Undosh + y bo‘lsa, y → ies bo‘ladi (baby → babies). Unli + y bo‘lsa, faqat -s qo‘shiladi (boy → boys). -f / -fe ko‘pincha -ves ga aylanadi (knife → knives).

**Practise**

1. [Choose] one baby, two ___ — babys / **babies** / babyes  
   *Hint: b + y: change y to i and add -es.*
2. [Choose] one boy, three ___ — **boys** / boies  
   *Hint: o + y (a vowel + y): just add -s.*
3. [Write] one city, two ___ → **cities**  
   *Hint: t + y: change y to i and add -es.*
4. [Write] one knife, four ___ → **knives**  
   *Hint: -fe changes to -ves.*
5. [Correct sentence] **The keys are on the table.** ✓ · The keies are on the table. ✗  
   *Hint: e + y (a vowel + y): just add -s.*

### Part 3: Special plurals

Some nouns have a special plural. Learn them by heart: man → men, woman → women, child → children, person → people, foot → feet, tooth → teeth, mouse → mice. A few nouns are the same in the plural: one sheep → two sheep, one fish → two fish.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| man | men |
| woman | women |
| child | children |
| person | people |
| foot | feet |
| tooth | teeth |
| mouse | mice |
| sheep / fish | sheep / fish |

Examples:

- one man → two men
- a child → three children
- The children are in the garden.
- Many people are in the shop.
- My feet are cold.
- Two sheep are in the field.

🇺🇿 Ba’zi otlarning ko‘pligi qoidaga bo‘ysunmaydi, ularni yodlab olish kerak: child — children, man — men, person — people.

**Practise**

1. [Choose] one child, two ___ — childs / **children** / childrens  
   *Hint: child → children (a special plural).*
2. [Choose] one man, three ___ — mans / **men** / mens  
   *Hint: man → men (a special plural).*
3. [Write] one tooth, two ___ → **teeth**  
   *Hint: tooth → teeth, like foot → feet.*
4. [Write] one woman, five ___ → **women**  
   *Hint: woman → women, like man → men.*
5. [Correct sentence] **Two people are at the door.** ✓ · Two peoples are at the door. ✗ · Two people is at the door. ✗  
   *Hint: person → people (already plural, so use are).*

## Lesson 4 — This, That, These, Those

### Part 1: this and these (near)

Use this for one thing near you (here). Use these for two or more things near you. this + a singular noun: this book. these + a plural noun: these books.

| Near me (here) | Example |
|---|---|
| this + one thing | this book, this apple |
| these + two or more | these books, these apples |

Examples:

- This is my pen.
- This bag is heavy.
- These are my friends, Tom and Emma.
- These shoes are new.
- Is this your phone?

🇺🇿 this — bu (bitta, yaqindagi narsa), these — bular (ko‘p, yaqindagi narsalar).

**Practise**

1. [Choose] ___ is my book. — **This** / These  
   *Hint: One thing near you: this.*
2. [Choose] ___ are my pencils. — This / **These**  
   *Hint: Two or more things near you: these.*
3. [Choose] ___ apples are green. — This / **These**  
   *Hint: Apples = more than one, so use these.*

   *Hint: One thing near you: this.*
5. [Correct sentence] **These are my keys.** ✓ · This are my keys. ✗ · These is my keys. ✗  
   *Hint: Keys = more than one: these + are.*

### Part 2: that and those (far)

Use that for one thing far from you (there). Use those for two or more things far from you. that + a singular noun: that house. those + a plural noun: those houses.

|  | One | Two or more |
|---|---|---|
| Near (here) | this | these |
| Far (there) | that | those |

Examples:

- That is our school.
- That man is my uncle.
- Those are my parents.
- Those birds are very small.
- Is that your car over there?

🇺🇿 that — u (bitta, uzoqdagi narsa), those — ular (ko‘p, uzoqdagi narsalar).

   *Hint: Two or more people far away: those.*

   *Hint: Girls = more than one: those girls.*

### Part 3: What's this? What are those?

When you ask about this or that, answer with it: What's this? — It's a clock. When you ask about these or those, answer with they: What are these? — They're keys. Short answers: Yes, it is. / No, it isn't. Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What's this? / What's that? | It's a pen. |
| What are these? / What are those? | They're pens. |
| Is this your bag? | Yes, it is. / No, it isn't. |
| Are those your shoes? | Yes, they are. / No, they aren't. |

Examples:

- What's this? — It's a map.
- What are these? — They're grapes.
- Who is that? — That's my brother.
- Is that your coat? — Yes, it is.
- Are these your glasses? — No, they aren't.

🇺🇿 this / that haqidagi savolga it bilan, these / those haqidagi savolga they bilan javob beriladi: What are these? — They're keys.

## Lesson 5 — Have Got / Has Got

### Part 1: I have got, she has got

Use have got / has got to say what someone owns, and to talk about family and how someone looks. Use have got with I, you, we, they. Use has got with he, she, it. Short forms: I've got, you've got, he's got, she's got.

| Person | Full form | Short form |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | have got | I've got / you've got / we've got / they've got |

## Lesson 8 — Countable and Uncountable Nouns

### Part 1: Can you count it?

Countable nouns are things you can count: one apple, two apples. They have a plural, and you can use a / an with one. Uncountable nouns are things you can't count, like water, milk, rice, bread, money, sugar and music. They have no plural and no a / an, and they go with is: The water is cold.

| Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|
| an apple, two apples | water |
| a banana, bananas | milk |
| an egg, eggs | bread |
| a bottle, bottles | rice |
| a coin, coins | money |

Examples:

- I've got an apple.
- There are three eggs in the fridge.
- The milk is in the fridge.
- Rice is good for you.
- My money is in my bag.

🇺🇿 Sanaladigan otlar (apple — olma) ko‘plikda -s oladi va a / an bilan keladi. Sanalmaydigan otlar (water — suv, rice — guruch) -s ham, a / an ham olmaydi.

To count uncountable things, use a word for a container or an amount: a bottle of water, a cup of tea, a piece of cake, a kilo of rice. For more than one, put -s on the container word: two cups of tea (not two cup of teas).

## Lesson 13 — Adverbs of Frequency

### Part 1: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never

Adverbs of frequency say how often something happens. always = every time. never = not at any time. The others are in between: usually, often, sometimes, rarely.

| Word | How often |
|---|---|
| always | 100% — every time |
| usually | about 90% |
| often | about 70% |
| sometimes | about 50% |
| rarely | about 10% |
| never | 0% — not at any time |

Examples:

- I always brush my teeth in the morning.
- We usually have dinner at seven.
- Tom often plays computer games.
- I sometimes walk to school.
- My dad rarely watches TV.
- Anna never drinks coffee.

🇺🇿 Bu so‘zlar ish qanchalik tez-tez bo‘lishini bildiradi: always — doim, usually — odatda, often — tez-tez, sometimes — ba’zan, rarely — kamdan-kam, never — hech qachon.

**Practise**

1. [Choose] I brush my teeth every morning and every evening. I ___ brush my teeth. — **always** / never  
   *Hint: Every time = always.*
2. [Choose] Sam doesn't like fish. He ___ eats fish. — always / **never**  
   *Hint: Not at any time = never.*
3. [Choose] Which word means about 50%: at some times, but not always? — **sometimes** / never / always  
   *Hint: About 50% = sometimes.*
4. [Choose] Kate goes swimming five days a week. She ___ goes swimming. — **often** / never / rarely  
   *Hint: Five days a week is a lot = often.*
5. [Write] I go to the cinema once a year. I ___ go to the cinema. (rarely / always) → **rarely**  
   *Hint: Once a year is not much = rarely.*

### Part 2: Where does it go?

The adverb goes before the main verb: I often play tennis. But it goes after am, is, are: She is always happy. In a negative, it goes after don't / doesn't: I don't usually eat meat.

|  | Where | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a main verb (play, eat, go…) | before the verb | I often play tennis. |
| am / is / are | after it | She is always happy. |
| don't / doesn't | after it | I don't usually eat meat. |

Examples:

- I usually get up at seven.
- My mum often cooks rice.
- We are never late for school.
- He is sometimes tired after work.
- They always visit their grandmother on Sunday.

🇺🇿 Bu so‘zlar asosiy fe’ldan oldin keladi (I often play), lekin am, is, are dan keyin keladi (She is always happy).

**Practise**

   *Hint: The adverb goes before the main verb (get up).*
2. [Correct sentence] **She is never late.** ✓ · She never is late. ✗ · Never she is late. ✗  
   *Hint: The adverb goes after is.*

   *Hint: We + always + walk (the adverb before the verb).*

   *Hint: He + is + often (the adverb after is).*
5. [Correct sentence] **Anna never drinks coffee.** ✓ · Anna drinks never coffee. ✗ · Anna never drink coffee. ✗  
   *Hint: never goes before the verb, and Anna = she, so drinks.*

### Part 3: How often? Once a week, twice a week

Ask How often…? to find out how many times. Answer with an adverb, or with every day, once a week (one time), twice a week (two times), three times a month. These go at the end of the sentence. Never is already negative, so don't use it with don't: I never eat meat (not I don't never eat meat).

| How many times? | We say |
|---|---|
| 1 time | once a day / once a week |
| 2 times | twice a week / twice a month |
| 3 or more times | three times a week / four times a year |
| each day | every day |

Examples:

- How often do you go to the gym? — Three times a week.
- I visit my grandparents every Sunday.
- She calls her mum once a day.
- We have English twice a week.
- I never drink cola.

🇺🇿 How often…? — Qanchalik tez-tez…? Javob: every day — har kuni, once a week — haftada bir marta, twice a week — haftada ikki marta.

   *Hint: two times = twice.*
4. [Correct sentence] **I never eat meat.** ✓ · I don't never eat meat. ✗  
   *Hint: never is already negative: don't use it with don't.*
5. [Put in order] often / How / you / do / read → **How often do you read?**  
   *Hint: How often + do + you + read?*

## Lesson 14 — Object Pronouns + Possessive Pronouns

### Part 1: me, him, her, us, them

Object pronouns come after a verb (help, like, see…) or after a preposition (with, for, to, at). I → me, you → you, he → him, she → her, it → it, we → us, they → them.

| Subject (before the verb) | Object (after the verb) |
|---|---|
| I | me |
| you | you |
| he | him |
| she | her |
| it | it |
| we | us |
| they | them |

Examples:

- Can you help me?
- Tom is my friend. I play football with him.
- This is Anna. I often see her at school.
- This song is great. I love it.
- Our teacher helps us a lot.
- My grandparents are kind. I visit them every week.

🇺🇿 Bu olmoshlar fe’l yoki predlogdan keyin keladi: help me — menga yordam ber, with him — u bilan, I love it — Men uni yaxshi ko‘raman.

**Practise**

1. [Choose] This is my friend Sam. I like ___. — he / **him** / his  
   *Hint: After a verb (like), he → him.*
2. [Choose] Lucy is nice. I often talk to ___. — she / **her** / hers  
   *Hint: After to, she → her.*
3. [Choose] Please wait for ___. I'm coming! — I / **me** / my  
   *Hint: After for, I → me.*
4. [Write] My parents are great. I love ___. (they) → **them**  
   *Hint: After a verb (love), they → them.*
5. [Correct sentence] **The teacher helps us.** ✓ · The teacher helps we. ✗ · The teacher helps our. ✗  
   *Hint: After a verb (helps), we → us.*

### Part 2: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs

Possessive pronouns stand alone — no noun comes after them: This book is mine (= my book). Compare: my bag → It's mine. her pen → It's hers. There is no apostrophe in yours, hers, ours or theirs.

| Before a noun | Alone |
|---|---|
| my bag | mine |
| your bag | yours |
| his bag | his |
| her bag | hers |
| our bag | ours |
| their bag | theirs |

Examples:

- This phone is mine.
- Is this pen yours?
- That isn't my coat. It's hers.
- The red car is ours.
- These books aren't theirs.
- My bike is old, but his is new.

🇺🇿 mine, yours, hers, ours, theirs — “meniki, seniki, uniki, bizniki, ularniki” degani. Ulardan keyin ot kelmaydi: This is mine — Bu meniki.

**Practise**

1. [Choose] This is my bag. It's ___. — my / **mine** / me  
   *Hint: No noun after it: my → mine.*
2. [Choose] Is this your pen? Is it ___? — your / **yours** / you  
   *Hint: No noun after it: your → yours.*
3. [Choose] That's Anna's phone. It's ___. — her / **hers** / she  
   *Hint: No noun after it: her → hers.*
4. [Write] This is our house. It's ___. (we) → **ours**  
   *Hint: No noun after it: our → ours.*
5. [Correct sentence] **These shoes are theirs.** ✓ · These shoes are their's. ✗ · These shoes are their. ✗  
   *Hint: theirs has no apostrophe.*

### Part 3: Whose is it? — It's Tom's.

Ask Whose…? to find out who something belongs to. Answer with a name + 's (Tom's) or with mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs. When the noun is plural and ends in -s, add only an apostrophe: my parents' car (= the car of my parents).

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Whose bag is this? | It's mine. / It's Tom's. |
| Whose shoes are these? | They're hers. / They're Emma's. |
| Whose car is that? | It's my parents' car. |

Examples:

- Whose bag is this? — It's mine.
- Whose is this coat? — It's Emma's.
- This is my sister's room.
- Whose are these shoes? — They're Sam's.
- My parents' house is near the park.

🇺🇿 Whose…? — Kimning…? Javobda ism + 's (Tom's — Tomniki) yoki mine, hers kabi so‘zlar ishlatiladi.

1. [Choose] ___ book is this? — Who / **Whose** / Who's

   *Hint: Name + 's: Tom's.*
3. [Write] Whose cat is this? — It's ___. (= my cat, one word) → **mine**  
   *Hint: my cat → mine.*
4. [Correct sentence] **This is my brother's room.** ✓ · This is my brothers room. ✗ · This is my brother room. ✗  
   *Hint: Name or noun + 's: my brother's room.*
5. [Put in order] coat / Whose / this / is → **Whose coat is this?** or **Whose is this coat?**  
   *Hint: Whose + coat + is + this?*

## Lesson 15 — Imperatives and Basic Sentence Structure

### Part 1: Open the door. Don't run.

Use the imperative to give instructions, orders or advice. Start with the verb as it is — don't say you: Open your books. Sit down. For a negative, use Don't + verb: Don't run. Add please to be polite: Please sit down. / Sit down, please.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Open the window. | Don't open the window. |
| Sit down, please. | Don't sit there. |
| Be quiet. | Don't be late. |

Examples:

- Open your books at page 10.
- Listen and repeat.
- Please sit down.
- Don't talk in the library.
- Be careful! The floor is wet.
- Don't forget your homework.

🇺🇿 Buyruq gapda you aytilmaydi, gap fe’l bilan boshlanadi: Sit down — O‘tiring. Inkor: Don't + fe’l: Don't run — Yugurmang.

**Practise**

1. [Choose] ___ the door, please. It's cold. — **Close** / Closes / Closing  
   *Hint: An instruction starts with the verb as it is: Close.*
2. [Choose] ___ late for school! — **Don't be** / Not be / Don't  
   *Hint: Negative: Don't + verb (be).*
3. [Write] ___ touch the oven. It's hot! (negative) → **Don't** / **Do not**  
   *Hint: Negative: Don't + verb.*
4. [Correct sentence] **Please be quiet.** ✓ · Please are quiet. ✗ · Please you be quiet. ✗  
   *Hint: Start with the verb as it is (be), with no you.*
5. [Put in order] your / Open / books → **Open your books.**  
   *Hint: Start with the verb: Open.*

### Part 2: Subject + verb + object

An English sentence usually goes like this: subject (who?) + verb (does what?) + object (what? / who?). I like apples. The verb comes right after the subject, not at the end of the sentence.

| Subject (who?) | Verb (does what?) | Object (what?) |
|---|---|---|
| I | like | apples. |
| My sister | reads | books. |
| We | play | football. |

Examples:

- Tom eats an apple.
- My parents love music.
- The children watch cartoons.
- She speaks three languages.
- We visit our grandparents.

🇺🇿 Ingliz tilida gap tartibi: ega + fe’l + to‘ldiruvchi (I like apples). O‘zbek tilida fe’l gap oxirida keladi (Men olmani yaxshi ko‘raman), ingliz tilida esa egadan keyin keladi.

**Practise**

1. [Correct sentence] **I like music.** ✓ · I music like. ✗ · Like I music. ✗  
   *Hint: Subject + verb + object: I + like + music.*
2. [Correct sentence] **Anna reads books.** ✓ · Anna books reads. ✗ · Reads Anna books. ✗  
   *Hint: The verb comes right after the subject: Anna reads.*
3. [Put in order] football / We / play → **We play football.**  
   *Hint: We (subject) + play (verb) + football (object).*
4. [Put in order] milk / The / cat / drinks → **The cat drinks milk.**  
   *Hint: The cat (subject) + drinks (verb) + milk (object).*
5. [Put in order] English / teaches / Mrs / Green → **Mrs Green teaches English.**  
   *Hint: Mrs Green (subject) + teaches (verb) + English (object).*

### Part 3: Where and when go at the end

After the object, say where (the place), then when (the time): I play football (what) in the park (where) on Saturdays (when). Words like every day, on Sundays and after school don't go between the subject and the verb.

| Subject | Verb | Object | Where? | When? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | play | football | in the park | on Saturdays. |
| She | has | lunch | at school | every day. |
| We | watch | TV | at home | in the evening. |

Examples:

- I meet my friends in the cafe on Fridays.
- My dad reads the newspaper at home every morning.
- They play tennis in the park after school.
- Tom does his homework in his room in the evening.
- We have English lessons at school on Mondays.

🇺🇿 Gap oxirida avval joy (qayerda?), keyin vaqt (qachon?) aytiladi: I play football in the park on Saturdays.

**Practise**

1. [Correct sentence] **I go to the gym every day.** ✓ · I every day go to the gym. ✗ · Go I to the gym every day. ✗  
   *Hint: The time (every day) goes at the end.*
2. [Correct sentence] **We eat lunch at school every day.** ✓ · We every day eat lunch at school. ✗ · We eat at school every day lunch. ✗  
   *Hint: Subject + verb + object + where + when.*
3. [Put in order] in / I / play / the / park / football → **I play football in the park.**  
   *Hint: I + play + football + in the park (where).*
4. [Put in order] on / Sundays / They / their / grandmother / visit → **They visit their grandmother on Sundays.**  
   *Hint: They + visit + their grandmother + on Sundays (when).*
5. [Correct sentence] **My brother works in a bank.** ✓ · My brother in a bank works. ✗ · In a bank works my brother. ✗  
   *Hint: Subject + verb, then the place: My brother works in a bank.*
