# Step 1 --- A Student's Daily Routine

**Target duration:** 4 minutes\
**Audio format:** Short talk\
**Questions:** 10

## Audio transcript

Hello! My name is Daniel, and I'm a student. Today, I'm going to tell
you about my daily routine.

On weekdays, I usually wake up at seven o'clock. I don't like getting up
early, especially when the weather is cold, but I have to get ready for
school. First, I wash my face and brush my teeth. Then I get dressed and
go to the kitchen. My mother usually prepares breakfast for the family.
I normally have eggs, bread, and tea. Sometimes I eat cereal with milk
if I don't have much time.

I leave home at about half past seven. My school is not very far away,
so I usually walk there. It takes around fifteen minutes. I like walking
because I can get some fresh air before my lessons start. If it rains,
my father sometimes drives me to school.

Lessons begin at eight o'clock. I study different subjects, including
English, mathematics, history, and science. English is my favourite
subject because I enjoy learning new words and speaking with my
classmates. Mathematics is a little more difficult for me, but I try to
practise it every day.

We have a short break at ten thirty. I usually talk to my friends or buy
something small to eat. Our lunch break is at twelve thirty. I often
bring a sandwich and some fruit from home, but sometimes I eat lunch in
the school cafeteria.

School finishes at two thirty. When I get home, I have lunch if I'm
hungry and then take a short rest. After that, I do my homework. I
usually spend about one hour on it, but some days I need more time.

In the evening, I like listening to music, watching videos, or playing
computer games. I also help my parents with small jobs around the house.
For example, I sometimes wash the dishes or tidy my room.

We usually have dinner together at seven thirty. After dinner, I prepare
my school bag and choose my clothes for the next day. I don't want to do
these things in the morning because I'm often in a hurry.

Before going to bed, I sometimes read a few pages of a book. I usually
go to bed at ten thirty. I know that getting enough sleep is important,
so I try not to stay up late.

That's my usual weekday. My weekends are quite different because I don't
have to go to school.

# Step 2 --- A Family Conversation

**Target duration:** 4 minutes\
**Audio format:** Conversation between two friends

## Audio transcript

**Emma:** Hi, Jack! How was your weekend?

**Jack:** It was great, thanks. I spent most of it with my family. We
had a small family lunch on Sunday.

**Emma:** That sounds nice. How many people are there in your family?

**Jack:** There are five of us: my parents, my older sister, my younger
brother, and me. My grandparents live in another town, but they visit us
quite often.

**Emma:** Do you get along well with your brother and sister?

**Jack:** Most of the time! My sister is twenty, so she's older than me
by three years. She's quite responsible and always helps me with my
studies. My younger brother is only eight. He's funny and energetic, but
sometimes he makes a lot of noise.

**Emma:** I have a younger sister too. She's ten, and we share a
bedroom. Sometimes we argue because she takes my things without asking.

**Jack:** I understand that! My brother sometimes uses my headphones. I
tell him to ask me first, but he forgets.

**Emma:** What do your parents do?

**Jack:** My father is an engineer. He works in an office and usually
comes home at six. My mother is a teacher. She works at a primary school
not far from our house.

**Emma:** Does your mother help you with your homework?

**Jack:** Sometimes, especially with English. But my sister is better at
mathematics, so I usually ask her for help with that subject.

**Emma:** What do you usually do together as a family?

**Jack:** We often have dinner together. At weekends, we sometimes watch
a film or visit my grandparents. My father also enjoys cooking, so he
prepares special meals on Sundays.

**Emma:** What did you do at your family lunch yesterday?

**Jack:** My grandmother made her famous chicken soup, and my father
prepared a big dish of plov. We also had a salad and some fruit. After
lunch, we sat in the garden and talked.

**Emma:** That sounds lovely. I don't see my grandparents very often
because they live far away.

**Jack:** You should call them more often. My grandmother really enjoys
talking on the phone. She always asks me about school and my friends.

**Emma:** You're right. I'll call them this evening.

**Jack:** That's a good idea. Family is important, even when everyone is
busy.

# Step 3 --- Finding a New House

**Target duration:** 4 minutes\
**Audio format:** Conversation between a customer and an estate agent

## Audio transcript

**Agent:** Good morning. Welcome to Green Homes. How can I help you?

**Michael:** Good morning. I'm looking for a house to rent. My family
and I want to move next month.

**Agent:** Of course. How many bedrooms do you need?

**Michael:** Three bedrooms would be perfect. We have two children, and
they need their own space. We would also like a small garden.

**Agent:** I see. Do you have a monthly budget?

**Michael:** We'd prefer to pay around eight hundred dollars a month. We
could pay a little more for the right house, but we don't want anything
too expensive.

**Agent:** I have two possible houses. The first one is in the city
centre. It has three bedrooms, a large kitchen, and a comfortable living
room. There is a supermarket across the street, and the bus stop is only
two minutes away.

**Michael:** That sounds convenient. Does it have a garden?

**Agent:** Unfortunately, no. It has a small balcony instead. The rent
is seven hundred and fifty dollars per month.

**Michael:** Hmm. We really want a garden because our children enjoy
playing outside. What about the second house?

**Agent:** The second house is in a quieter neighbourhood, about twenty
minutes from the city centre by bus. It has three bedrooms, two
bathrooms, and a lovely garden. There is also a small park nearby.

**Michael:** That sounds much better. Is there a supermarket in the
area?

**Agent:** Yes. There is one about five minutes away on foot. There is
also a primary school near the park.

**Michael:** Excellent. How much is the rent?

**Agent:** Eight hundred and twenty dollars per month. Water and heating
are included, but electricity is not.

**Michael:** That's a little above our budget, but it might be possible.
Is the house furnished?

**Agent:** It has a sofa, a dining table, and some kitchen cupboards.
However, there are no beds or wardrobes.

**Michael:** That's fine. We have our own beds. Can we visit the house
this week?

**Agent:** Certainly. I have an appointment available on Thursday at
four in the afternoon.

**Michael:** Thursday at four works for me. I'll come with my wife.

**Agent:** Great. Could I have your phone number so I can confirm the
appointment?

**Michael:** Of course. I'll give it to you now.

# Step 4 --- Food Around the World

**Target duration:** 4 minutes\
**Audio format:** Informative talk\
**Questions:** 11

## Audio transcript

Hello, everyone! Today, we're going to talk about food around the world.
Food is an important part of every culture. People in different
countries eat different meals, use different ingredients, and have their
own cooking traditions.

Let's start with Italy. Italian food is famous in many countries. Pizza
and pasta are two of the most popular dishes. Italian people often use
tomatoes, cheese, olive oil, and fresh vegetables. Many families enjoy
eating together, especially at weekends. A traditional Italian meal can
take quite a long time because people like talking while they eat.

Now, let's travel to Japan. Rice is an important part of Japanese food.
Many people eat rice with fish, vegetables, and soup. Sushi is one of
the most famous Japanese dishes. It usually contains rice and fish,
although there are different types. Japanese meals are often simple and
carefully prepared.

In Mexico, food is usually full of flavour. Tacos are very popular.
People put meat, vegetables, cheese, or beans inside small tortillas.
Some Mexican dishes are spicy because cooks use different kinds of
peppers. However, not every Mexican meal is hot. There are also many
sweet dishes and desserts.

Let's visit India. Indian food is known for its spices. People use
ingredients such as rice, potatoes, tomatoes, and different spices.
Curry is a popular dish, and there are many different versions. Some
people eat meat, while others prefer vegetarian meals. In fact,
vegetarian food is very common in many parts of India.

Finally, let's talk about Uzbekistan. Uzbek cuisine has a long history
and many traditional dishes. Plov is probably the most famous one. It is
usually made with rice, meat, carrots, onions, and oil. People often
prepare it for family celebrations and special events. Other popular
dishes include somsa, manti, and shashlik.

Although food is different around the world, it brings people together.
Families share meals, friends meet at restaurants, and people celebrate
important moments with special dishes.

So, next time you try food from another country, remember that you are
also learning something about its culture. Thank you for listening!

# Step 5 --- Weekend Plans

**Target duration:** 5 minutes\
**Audio format:** Conversation between two friends\
**Questions:** 12

## Audio transcript

**Emma:** Hi, Daniel! Are you busy this Saturday?

**Daniel:** Not really. Why are you asking?

**Emma:** My cousin is visiting us this weekend. I want to show her
around the city, but I don't know where to take her.

**Daniel:** That's nice! What's her name?

**Emma:** Her name is Sophie. She's seventeen, and she's really
interested in photography. She loves taking pictures of interesting
buildings and beautiful places.

**Daniel:** Then you should take her to the old part of the city. There
are some beautiful historical buildings there. The streets are quite
narrow, but they look amazing in photographs.

**Emma:** That's a good idea. Is there anything else we can do?

**Daniel:** Well, there's a new art exhibition at the city museum. It
opened last Monday. My sister went there yesterday, and she said it was
really interesting.

**Emma:** Does it cost much?

**Daniel:** No, the tickets are quite cheap. I think they're around five
dollars for students. Adults pay eight dollars.

**Emma:** Perfect! What about lunch? I don't want to spend the whole day
walking around without eating anything.

**Daniel:** There's a small Italian restaurant near the museum. I went
there with my family last month. The food was delicious, and the prices
were reasonable.

**Emma:** Does it serve vegetarian food? Sophie doesn't eat meat.

**Daniel:** Yes, they have several vegetarian dishes. My sister ordered
a vegetable pizza, and she really enjoyed it.

**Emma:** Great. Maybe we can invite some other friends too.

**Daniel:** Sure. What about Mark and Lisa? They usually have free time
on Saturdays.

**Emma:** I'll send them a message tonight. By the way, what time should
we meet?

**Daniel:** How about nine thirty in the morning? We can meet in front
of the central library.

**Emma:** Isn't that a little early? I usually wake up at nine on
Saturdays!

**Daniel:** Come on, Emma! We have a lot to see. But we can make it ten
o'clock if you prefer.

**Emma:** Ten is much better. I'll probably take a taxi because the bus
is always crowded in the morning.

**Daniel:** I think I'll come by bicycle. The weather forecast says it
will be sunny, so it should be a pleasant ride.

**Emma:** Excellent. I'll bring my camera, and Sophie can use my old
phone to take pictures too.

**Daniel:** Sounds like a plan! Don't forget to charge your camera
tonight.

**Emma:** I won't. See you on Saturday!

**Daniel:** See you!

# Step 6 --- Two Friends Talking

**Target duration:** 5 minutes\
**Audio format:** Conversation\
**Questions:** 13

## Audio transcript

**Oliver:** Hey, Sam! I haven't seen you for a few days. How are things?

**Sam:** Pretty good, thanks. I've been quite busy recently. I've
started going to a new sports club.

**Oliver:** Really? What kind of sport do you do there?

**Sam:** I play basketball three times a week. I've always liked
basketball, but I never had enough time to practise regularly.

**Oliver:** That's great! Do you play with your school friends?

**Sam:** Sometimes. But the club has different groups. My group has
twelve people, and most of them are around my age. Our coach is very
friendly, but he expects us to work hard.

**Oliver:** How long are your training sessions?

**Sam:** About ninety minutes. We usually start with some simple
exercises to warm up. Then we practise passing, shooting, and working as
a team. At the end, we play a short match.

**Oliver:** That sounds fun. I used to play basketball when I was
younger, but I stopped because I didn't have a good team.

**Sam:** You should join us! We need one more player for our Saturday
group.

**Oliver:** I'd love to, but I'm not very good at basketball anymore.

**Sam:** Don't worry about that. Nobody expects you to be perfect. The
important thing is to practise and enjoy the game.

**Oliver:** You're right. How much does the club cost?

**Sam:** It's thirty dollars a month. You can also pay for three months
together, which is a little cheaper.

**Oliver:** That's not too expensive. Do I need to bring anything?

**Sam:** Just sports clothes, comfortable trainers, and a bottle of
water. They have basketballs at the club, so you don't need to buy one.

**Oliver:** Nice. Where is it?

**Sam:** It's next to the city swimming pool, about ten minutes from the
central bus station. You can take bus number six. It stops right outside
the sports centre.

**Oliver:** I know that area. What time do you train on Saturdays?

**Sam:** Our session starts at eleven in the morning. But I usually
arrive fifteen minutes early because I like to get ready before
training.

**Oliver:** I think I'll come with you this Saturday. Can I watch first?

**Sam:** Of course! You can watch the first half, and then you can join
us if you want.

**Oliver:** Great. By the way, do you do any other activities?

**Sam:** I also go swimming on Sundays. It's a good way to relax after a
busy week. I used to spend most of my weekends playing computer games,
but now I prefer doing something active.

**Oliver:** I should probably do the same. I spend too much time sitting
at home.

**Sam:** You don't have to change everything at once. Just start with
one activity you enjoy.

**Oliver:** Good advice. I'll see you on Saturday, then.

**Sam:** See you! And don't forget your water bottle!

# Step 7 --- A School Announcement

**Target duration:** 5 minutes\
**Audio format:** School announcement\
**Questions:** 13

## Audio transcript

Good morning, students!

I have some important information about activities at our school next
week. Please listen carefully and write down anything you need to
remember.

First, let's talk about the school library. From Monday, the library
will open at eight thirty in the morning instead of nine. It will close
at four thirty in the afternoon. Students can borrow up to three books
at a time. You can keep each book for two weeks. If you need more time,
you can ask the librarian to extend the borrowing period.

Next, we are organising a school sports day on Wednesday. It will take
place on the school playground, starting at ten o'clock. There will be
several activities, including football, basketball, volleyball, and
running competitions. Students who want to take part should give their
names to their class teachers before Tuesday afternoon. Remember to wear
comfortable sports clothes and bring a bottle of water.

Now, some information about the English club. We have decided to change
the meeting day. The club usually meets on Thursday, but next week it
will meet on Friday at three fifteen. The meeting will be in Room 12,
not Room 8. We will watch a short film and discuss the characters and
the story. You don't need to prepare anything before the meeting.

There is also a science exhibition in the main hall next Thursday.
Students from different classes have prepared small projects about
animals, plants, and the environment. The exhibition will open at nine
in the morning and finish at one o'clock. You can visit it with your
classmates during your free lesson. Please remember that food and drinks
are not allowed in the exhibition area.

Finally, I want to remind everyone about the school trip. We are going
to the history museum on Friday, the twenty-first of this month. The bus
will leave from the school entrance at eight fifteen, so please arrive
at least fifteen minutes early. The journey will take about forty
minutes. You should bring a packed lunch, a notebook, and a pen. The
school will pay for the bus, but students need to bring money for their
museum tickets. The ticket costs four dollars.

If you have any questions about these activities, please speak to your
class teacher. Thank you for listening, and have a good day!
