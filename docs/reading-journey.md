# Reading Journey — 20 Steps

A1+ → A2. The texts grow from about 185 words (Step 1) to about 600 (Step 20).  
Steps 1–10: True or False + questions (odd steps) or + gap filling (even steps). Steps 11–20: True / False / Not given + gap filling + questions.

## Step 1 — A Day in My Life

### 1. Reading Text (185 words)

My name is Daniel, and I am a 17-year-old student. I usually wake up at 7 o'clock in the morning. First, I take a shower and get dressed. Then, I have breakfast with my family. I usually eat eggs and bread and drink a cup of tea.

I go to school at 8 o'clock. My lessons start at 8:30 and finish at 2 p.m. My favourite subjects are English and history because I enjoy learning new things. During the break, I talk to my classmates and sometimes play games with them.

After school, I come home and have lunch. Then, I do my homework and help my parents with some housework. In the evening, I sometimes watch movies, listen to music or go for a walk with my friends.

I usually have dinner at 8 p.m. After dinner, I spend some time with my family. Before going to bed, I read a book or watch a few videos. I usually go to bed at 10:30.

I like my daily routine because I have enough time for studying, relaxing and spending time with people I love.

## Step 4 — Traditional Food Around the World

In Japan, rice is part of almost every meal. People also eat a lot of fish and vegetables. Sushi is the most famous Japanese dish. It is made with rice, raw fish and seaweed.

Mexico is famous for tacos. A taco is a soft or crispy tortilla with meat, beans, cheese and spicy sauce. Many Mexicans eat tacos for breakfast, lunch and dinner!

In India, many dishes are full of spices. Curry is very popular, and people often eat it with rice or flat bread. In the south of India, some people eat with their right hand.

In Turkey, breakfast is a big meal with bread, cheese, olives, tomatoes, eggs and tea. People sit at the table for a long time and talk. Turkish people also love sweet desserts, and baklava, made with nuts and honey, is one of the most famous. Many visitors take a box of it home as a present.

Trying traditional food is one of the best ways to learn about a new culture.

## Step 6 — A True Friendship

### 1. Reading Text (282 words)

When Tom was ten, his family moved to a new town. On his first day at the new school, he felt nervous and lonely. Nobody talked to him during the break.

Then a boy with curly hair sat next to him. "Hi, I'm Ben," he said. "Do you want to play football with us?" Tom smiled. From that day, they became best friends.

Tom and Ben did everything together. They walked to school, did their homework and watched football matches on TV. Ben was always cheerful, and Tom was good at maths, so they helped each other.

When they were fifteen, Tom broke his leg while he was playing football. He stayed at home for six weeks. Every day after school, Ben visited him. He brought Tom's homework and told him all the school news. Tom never felt bored or alone.

There was one difficult time in their friendship. When they were seventeen, Ben started spending a lot of time with a new group of friends. He stopped calling Tom, and Tom felt hurt. For a few weeks, they didn't talk at all.

One evening, Tom decided to visit Ben and tell him how he felt. Ben was surprised. "I'm really sorry," he said. "I didn't want to lose you. I was just busy with my new friends." They talked for hours that night, and after that, their friendship became even stronger. Tom learned that good friends sometimes make mistakes, but they also say sorry.

Now Tom and Ben are twenty-five. They live in different cities, but they still call each other every week. Tom often says, "A true friend is someone who stays with you on good days and bad days."

## Step 8 — The Benefits of Having a Hobby

### 1. Reading Text (334 words)

A hobby is something you do in your free time because you enjoy it. Some people collect stamps, some play the guitar, and others grow plants. But did you know that hobbies are also good for us?

First, hobbies help us reduce stress. When you are busy with something you love, you forget about your problems for a while. For example, many people say that drawing or gardening makes them feel calm.

Second, hobbies help us learn new skills. A person who plays chess learns to think carefully. Someone who cooks learns to plan and to be creative. These skills are useful at school and at work too.

Third, hobbies help us make friends. When you join a club or a team, you meet people with the same interests. Emma, a 16-year-old student, joined a photography club last year. "Now I have five new friends," she says, "and we go out to take photos every Saturday."

Some hobbies can even become jobs. Harry Brooks, a 30-year-old from Leeds, started baking cakes as a hobby when he was a teenager. At first, he baked only for his family's birthdays. Then his friends started asking him to make cakes for their parties. Five years ago, he opened his own small bakery, and now he has three workers.

Of course, a hobby doesn't have to become a job. Doctors say it is important to do something just for fun. Many busy adults stop doing their hobbies because they have no time. But even thirty minutes a week can help.

If you want to start a hobby, think about what you enjoyed as a child. Did you like drawing, building things or dancing? You can also ask your friends what they do in their free time and join them. Remember that you don't need to be good at your hobby. The important thing is to enjoy it.

Finally, hobbies make life more interesting. So if you don't have a hobby yet, why not try something new this month?

Edinburgh is an old and beautiful city. On my first day, we climbed the hill to Edinburgh Castle. From the top, we could see the whole city and the sea. The next day, we visited a museum and walked along the Royal Mile, the most famous street in the old town.

The weather surprised me. It was July, but it was cold and windy, and it rained almost every afternoon. I had to buy a warm jacket!

I also tried some local food. One evening, my aunt cooked haggis, a traditional Scottish dish made with meat, onions and oats. I was not sure at first, but it was actually tasty. I also loved the shortbread, a sweet butter biscuit that people often eat with tea.

Every evening, I called my parents and told them about my day. They were happy because I sounded excited and not homesick. My mum said, "We are proud of you. You are more independent than we thought."

The hardest part of the trip was saying goodbye. On my last day, my aunt and I walked around the old town one more time, and I took a lot of photos. At the airport, I almost cried.

The trip changed me. I became more confident, and I discovered that I love exploring new places. Next year, I am planning to visit my aunt again.

## Step 20 — Four Seasons of the Year

### 1. Reading Text (582 words)

Why do we have seasons? Many people think it is because the Earth is closer to the Sun in summer. In fact, the reason is different. The Earth is tilted, so during the year, different parts of the planet get more or less sunlight.

In spring, the days get longer and warmer. Trees grow new leaves, flowers appear, and many birds return from warmer countries. Farmers start planting their crops.

Summer is the hottest season. The sun rises early and sets late, so we have long evenings. Schools close for the holidays, and many families go to the beach or the mountains.

In autumn, the weather becomes cooler, and leaves turn yellow, orange and red before they fall. Farmers harvest fruit and vegetables. Some animals, like squirrels, collect food for the winter.

Winter is the coldest season. In many places, it snows, and lakes freeze. Some animals, like bears, sleep for most of the winter.

Seasons are not the same everywhere. Near the equator, in countries like Indonesia or Kenya, it is warm all year, and there is no real winter. Instead, many of these places have two seasons: a dry season and a rainy season. During the rainy season, heavy rain falls almost every afternoon, and rivers can flood.

In the far north and the far south of the planet, the seasons are extreme. In the north of Norway, the sun does not set for about two months in summer. People call this the midnight sun. In winter, the opposite happens, and the sun does not rise for several weeks. People there use bright lamps at home to feel more awake and cheerful.

Animals and plants are ready for the seasons too. Many trees lose their leaves in autumn to save water during the cold months. Some birds fly thousands of kilometres to warmer countries before winter, and they find their way using the sun and the stars. Other animals, like the Arctic fox, change the colour of their fur: it is brown in summer and white in winter, so they can hide in the snow.

Seasons affect our bodies and moods too. Many people feel more energetic in spring and summer, when the days are long and sunny. In winter, some people feel tired and sad because there is less light. Doctors call this the 'winter blues'. Spending time outside during the day and staying active can help.

Seasons also shape our traditions. In Japan, people celebrate spring with hanami, when they have picnics under the cherry trees. In many countries, autumn is the time for harvest festivals, when people thank nature for the food. And in cold countries, winter is the season for skiing, ice skating and cosy evenings at home.

Interestingly, when it is winter in the north of the planet, it is summer in the south. In Australia, people often celebrate the New Year on the beach!

Our planet's climate is changing, and this is affecting the seasons. In many places, spring now starts earlier than it did fifty years ago, and summers are becoming hotter. Some winters have less snow, which is a problem for animals, plants and even for ski resorts. Scientists say we must protect the environment so that future generations can enjoy all four seasons.

Which season is your favourite? Some people love the warm days of summer, while others prefer the colours of autumn or the snowy landscapes of winter. Every season has its own beauty, and each one brings something special.
